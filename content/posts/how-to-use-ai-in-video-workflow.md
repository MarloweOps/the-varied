# How to Actually Use AI Tools in Your Video Production Workflow

Here's the thing about AI in production right now: everyone's asking "how do I use this?" when they should be asking "where do I *need* this?"

I've watched a lot of producers pick up AI tools, get excited for two weeks, then abandon them because they solved the wrong problem. The ones who are actually winning—who ship faster and keep their sanity—treat AI like any other tool: you only use it where it removes friction, not where it adds complexity.

This is how I've learned to integrate AI into real productions without it becoming a liability.

## Start with Your Pain Point, Not the Tool

Before you touch a single AI tool, identify what's slowing you down. Not what sounds cool. What actually costs you time and money.

For me, that was transcription. We'd finish a shoot and spend two days manually transcripting interviews and dialogue for editors to find usable takes. That's painful, repetitive work.

Then I looked at color grading setups—we'd spend hours creating a base grade on the first shot before the colorist could move fast. Another bottleneck.

Then asset organization. Every production coordinator I know spends half their day organizing footage by location, camera, subject.

These were concrete pain points. So these are where I brought in AI.

Compare that to "AI editing"—there's no pain point there for my workflows. Editing isn't the slow part. So I tested those tools, they were interesting, and then I moved on.

**Your first step:** Map your current workflow. Find the three tasks that eat the most time and add the least creative value. Those are your targets.

## Pre-Production: Script and Planning

This is where AI plays the smallest role, and that's intentional.

AI can help you generate script variations, explore different angles, or brainstorm angles quickly. Tools like ChatGPT or Claude are useful for this—you give them context about your brand, your product, your tone, and they'll generate options in minutes.

But here's the producer truth: AI-generated scripts need heavy editing. They don't know your voice, your humor, your specific references. You're not saving time by generating five scripts and picking one; you're generating five drafts and rewriting all of them.

**What actually works:** Use AI for brainstorming and variation generation, but treat every output as a rough draft. You're not using it to replace the writer; you're using it to ideate faster.

And be transparent: if you're using AI in scriptwriting, disclose it. Your audience doesn't care how you wrote it, but they'll know something's off if it sounds generic.

## Production: Automation and Intelligence at Capture

During the shoot, AI can handle what cameras are already doing—but smarter.

Some cameras now auto-adjust exposure and focus tracking. Some tools flag bad takes in real-time. Frame.io has started integrating Camera to Cloud workflows that move footage into review immediately with auto-tagging—so you're not manually organizing hundreds of clips after the shoot.

The value here is time. Not on set (your crew doesn't care about AI), but *immediately after*. Your assistant isn't spending the evening organizing footage by camera angle and location manually.

**Real implementation:** If you're using a modern camera and Frame.io, the integration happens automatically. You're not adding steps; you're replacing manual work with smart infrastructure.

The catch: this only works if your entire team is in Frame.io from day one. Half your team using it and half using Dropbox defeats the purpose.

## Post-Production: This Is Where AI Actually Works

Post is where AI saves real time and real money.

### Transcription First

This is the easiest win. Transcription is tedious, it's expensive if you hire it out, and AI tools are good enough now that they're *better* than manual transcription.

Tools like Descript, Adobe Premiere's built-in transcription, or Rev handle interviews, dialogue, and VO with 95%+ accuracy. Your editor can search dialogue, build a rough cut using transcription as a guide, and have subtitles ready the day after shooting instead of a week later.

Cost: $5-50 per hour of footage depending on the tool.
Time saved: 2-4 hours per shoot.
ROI: Easy. Next week.

### Color Grading Acceleration

Neural engines in DaVinci Resolve and Adobe's tools can analyze a shot and propose a grade in seconds. This isn't a finished grade. It's a starting point.

Here's the workflow: AI builds the foundation (white balance, exposure correction, basic contrast). Your colorist refines it from there. This cuts the "getting to 80% of the way there" stage from hours to minutes.

**Real impact:** A 10-hour color session becomes 7-8 hours. That's tangible.

Cost: Free (built into DaVinci Resolve) or part of Adobe subscriptions.
Time saved: 20-30% session reduction.
ROI: High, especially on color-heavy projects.

### Asset Organization and Search

This is unglamorous but hugely valuable. AI can automatically tag footage by subject, location, camera, and even emotion. Frame.io and other DAM tools are building this now.

Production coordinators spend hours organizing and re-organizing footage so editors can find what they need. AI can do this in minutes.

Your coordinator spends 2 hours on day 1 setting up tags. AI handles the rest. Editors spend 10 minutes searching instead of an hour digging through folders.

Cost: Part of Frame.io or Cantemo subscriptions.
Time saved: 2-4 hours per production.
ROI: High.

### Audio Cleanup and Localization

Bad location audio used to mean ADR (additional dialogue recording) or accepting rough sound. Now AI can clean up a lot of it.

Tools like iZotope and Adobe's built-in tools can remove wind noise, room tone, and hum. Not perfectly—you still need a good recordist on set—but enough to recover bad takes.

For localization, HeyGen and ElevenLabs can generate dubbed versions with AI voice, or realistic AI voiceovers for narration. This is useful for international projects or when you need multiple versions.

Cost: $50-500 depending on the tool and amount of content.
Time saved: 3-6 hours on audio cleanup, days if you need localization.
ROI: High for international projects, medium for domestic.

## Quality Control: AI Reviewing AI

This is newer but worth watching. Tools are starting to use AI to flag lip-sync issues, subtitle misalignments, and audio sync problems automatically. Instead of manually watching 30 minutes of content looking for issues, you get a report of potential problems.

Your QC person reviews the flagged items—maybe 10-15 minutes of work instead of 2 hours.

Cost: Usually built into production platforms.
Time saved: 30-60 minutes per deliverable.
ROI: Medium.

## Where AI Usually Fails

Before I finish, here's what doesn't work:

**"AI editing" / Auto-montage:** These tools create watchable rough cuts, not finished edits. You spend more time fixing them than starting from scratch.

**"AI directing" / Composition suggestions:** These miss emotional intent 90% of the time. Novelty, not essential.

**Fully generative shots:** "Just generate the shot you need" sounds great until you realize it wastes more time because the output never matches your other footage, color palette, or lighting.

**Replacing decision-makers:** AI that auto-selects takes, auto-cuts scenes, auto-grades footage sounds efficient until you realize you hate the choices. Automation isn't strategy.

## The Real Framework

Here's the framework I use for deciding whether to adopt any AI tool:

1. **Identify the pain point:** Something that's slow, repetitive, and low-creative-value.
2. **Measure the baseline:** How long does it take manually right now?
3. **Test the tool:** Run it on one shoot or one task.
4. **Calculate real ROI:** Is it 20%+ faster *including cleanup and learning curve*?
5. **Check consistency:** Would you trust this on a high-stakes broadcast shoot?

If it clears all five, you have a tool. If it fails any one, wait for the next generation.

## Integration Into OPA

Here's where this gets practical for how we manage production. Part of why I built OPA was to centralize the data around what's actually happening on set and in post—not just expenses, but metadata about which takes got approved, which assets are tagged, where time is getting lost.

When AI tools are tagging and organizing assets, and OPA is tracking how long tasks took and what data the AI flagged, you can actually see where AI is working and where it's not. This feedback loop is how you build smarter workflows, not just faster ones.

## The Honest Take

AI in production doesn't save time because it's magic. It saves time because it does the stuff nobody wants to do. Transcription is boring. Tagging 500 clips is boring. Setting up a color foundation is tedious.

Use AI for the boring stuff. Keep humans in charge of the creative decisions, the judgment calls, the moments that make something good instead of serviceable.

The producers who'll win in 2026 aren't the ones using the most AI. They're the ones using it strategically where it eliminates busywork, stays out of the way of creativity, and doesn't require a complete workflow rebuild to implement.

Pick your pain points. Test your tools. Measure real results. Then build from there.
