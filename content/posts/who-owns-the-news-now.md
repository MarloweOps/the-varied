I don't usually wade into media politics. But it's impossible to talk about the Paramount-WBD merger without mentioning that David Ellison spent $150 million to acquire The Free Press, installed Bari Weiss as editor-in-chief of CBS News, and is now about to own CNN too.

A veteran CBS News producer — 40 years at the network — just resigned because of what she called "instructions to aim our reporting at a particular part of the political spectrum." A "60 Minutes" segment on deportations got spiked after Trump officials refused to be interviewed. Sharyn Alfonsi, the correspondent, said publicly: "Not an editorial decision. A political one."

The same consolidation forces squeezing producers out of backend deals are concentrating media ownership in ways that would've been unthinkable ten years ago. The person who controls the studio also controls the news. That's not a production industry problem. That's an everyone problem.

I make content for a living. The line between entertainment and information is already blurry. When both flow through the same corporate structure, controlled by the same person, the blur becomes a feature, not a bug.

Worth paying attention to.
