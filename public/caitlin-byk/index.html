01 — Brand Essence

Meet Caitlin

Caitlin Byk is a 17-year-old pop artist from LA, freshly signed to Cash Money Republic. She has half a million streams on "Blow Away," and the kind of taste that rejects everything that tries too hard. The music is already working. Now the image catches up.

Positioning Statement

The LA pop girl who looks like she just rolled out of bed and into something iconic.

Effortless cool girl meets real musicianship — easy on the surface, deliberate underneath. She's the girl your audience wants to be friends with, and can't quite figure out how to be. Your cooler, slightly-more-rebellious bestie.

Two Modes

She's chill until she decides not to be. The flip is the brand.

Everyday

Real places, real light, candid

Elevated

Self-aware, kinetic, in your face

Everyday

Real places, real light, candid. Baggy jeans and a coffee. The version of her that makes you think "she's just like me." Warm, analog, no effort visible.

Elevated

Self-aware. She knows the camera's there and goes there. Kinetic, striking, in your face. The version that makes you think "I could never." Still her — just turned up.

"I want things to look effortlessly cool. Like I've been wasted the entire week and didn't try — but it should also look really good."

02 — Brand Pillars

Four Pillars

Pillar 01

Effortless Cool

The brand's center of gravity. Nothing can look like it's trying. Every visual, caption, outfit, and piece of content should feel like it happened naturally — even when it's meticulously produced. The effort is invisible.

"I'm a chill girl."

Styling Baggy baseline + one elevated piece (pointy heel, jacket, good bag)
Content Two buckets — everyday: real places, real light, candid moments. Elevated: self-aware, heightened reality, in your face visuals.
Voice Casual, unbothered, funny, slightly irreverent
Pillar 02

Y2K / Camcorder

Not a trend — a genuine aesthetic preference. This is her native visual language. Flash, grain, blown-out highlights. MiniDV texture, 4:3 ratios, datestamp overlays. Warm tones, amber, washed-out pastels, nothing clinical or cold.

"i'm so y2k. thats so my brand."

Photo Flash, grain, blown-out highlights, disposable camera feel
Video MiniDV / Hi8, 4:3 aspect ratio, datestamps, handheld shake
Design Lo-res type, early-internet warmth, anti-HD
Pillar 03

LA

Not Hollywood LA — neighborhood LA. Venice, DTLA, strip malls, poolside. The city as a character, not a backdrop. Sun-soaked, slightly reckless, main-character energy without the self-awareness.

"I like simple things. Sick ass outfit, my makeup is sick, I'm in a really chill place. That's me."

Settings Real locations — laundromats, diners, sidewalks, her pool, a parked car
Styling Street-casual baseline: baggy jeans, tanks, sneakers or pointy shoes
Energy Sun-soaked / party flash, main-character energy
Pillar 04

Edge

The brand sits between pop polish and something grittier. A bit grunge. Sweet but sharp. Will say exactly what she thinks.

"I hate all this trendy shit. I hate it. When things are very forced and like very try hard and trying to be cool — I hate it."

Music Pop melodies with production nods to hip-hop, R&B, electronic textures
Fashion Streetwear + high fashion coexisting (Acne jacket over a beater)
Grunge Smudged liner, torn hems, darker wash denim, vintage band tees. Not costume grunge — just a little undone.
Attitude Sweet but sharp. Honest. No filter.
03 — Photography

Photography

Two modes. Real locations only. Disposable camera energy — grain, flash, blown-out highlights. Nothing clinical, nothing studio.

Everyday

2003 disposable camera. Candid, warm, analog. Real light, real places. The version of her that makes you think "she's just like me."

Elevated

She knows the camera's there. Flash, contrast, kinetic energy. Movement blur, mid-spin, hair whip. Still with tension — like she's about to move.

The Feel
  • Real locations — pools, diners, sidewalks, parked cars, laundromats
  • Natural light or harsh disposable flash. Grain and blown-out highlights welcome.
  • Candid over posed. One prop max per scene.
Not This
  • No studio seamless. No clinical color grading. No teal-and-orange. No purple tones.
  • No dark/moody/underexposed. The brand is warm, even indoors.
  • No digital sharpness or HDR. Keep it soft and analog.

04 — Styling Rules

Wardrobe

The baseline is the brand. Baggy bottom, fitted top, one elevated piece. Every outfit should look like she threw it on without thinking — but the one nice thing makes you look twice.

"I like to wear baggy clothes. But like, adding in fun pieces — a jacket or like a crazy pair of pants — that takes it from everyday to artist Caitlin."

The Silhouette

Baggy + fitted + one statement piece.

This is the formula. Baggy jeans or wide pants as the base. Fitted or cropped top on top. One elevated accent — a structured jacket, a pointy heel, a designer bag, statement sunglasses. The accent is what bridges "relatable" and "unobtainable."

Do's

  • ✦ Baggy jeans — wide leg, carpenter, vintage wash
  • ✦ Pointy shoes with baggy pants
  • ✦ Layering — jackets, open shirts, interesting textures on top
  • ✦ Off-shoulder or asymmetric necklines (when casual, not costume-y)
  • ✦ Kitten heels with casual outfits — the high-low bridge
  • ✦ Vintage / secondhand pieces that feel lived-in
  • ✦ Simple hair and makeup — natural skin, undone hair

Don'ts

  • ✕ Jerseys — "fast fashion," hard no from Caitlin
  • ✕ Trend-forward pieces that will date in 6 months
  • ✕ Anything that reads as too put-together
  • ✕ Costumey maximalism
  • ✕ Full head-to-toe designer / editorial looks with no casual anchor

06 — Color Palette

Palette

Razr phone, lip gloss, cherry red nails, acid green camcorder text, icy blue flip phone wallpaper, chrome chain links. Hot pink is the signature. Cherry red is the heat. Icy blue is the cool flash. Acid lime is the era. Chrome is the texture. Five colors that don't ask permission — grounded by a warm black that keeps the whole thing from melting into candy.

Sidekick Pink
#E8368F
The color. The signature. Think Razr phone, lip gloss, the Juicy tracksuit. Hot, loud, unapologetic.
Bubblegum
#F06BAE
The softer pink. Still loud, just less urgent. The glow around the edges.
Cherry
#D72638
The red. Deeper, more dangerous than the pink — cherry red nails, a vintage Corvette, a stop sign at golden hour. Pink is playful. Cherry is serious.
Icy Blue
#6B8CFF
The cool flash. Periwinkle-meets-electric-blue — early-2000s web design, CD-ROM interfaces, flip phone wallpapers. A jolt of cool against the warm.
Datestamp
#B8E62E
Acid lime — straight from a 2003 camcorder timestamp. The Y2K tech accent. Metadata, timestamps, UI details.
Chrome
#C0C8D4
The metallic layer. Chrome hearts, silver chains, brushed aluminum. Borders, dividers, metallic text treatments.
Soot
#1A1513
Warm near-black. The anchor. Without this, the palette is a candy store. With it, it's a brand.

07 — Typography

Type

Dela Gothic One is a Japanese-designed ultra-black that feels like a streetwear poster, a vinyl sticker, a Y2K magazine cover all at once. The body font is soft and rounded. The mono is the camcorder layer.

Dela Gothic One

BLOW AWAY. SOME1 ELSE. TRUST.

UsageHeadlines, titles, wordmark, single art, merch, social
TreatmentUppercase always. Tight tracking. Go big.
Quicksand

She writes like a storyteller, not a vibe-chaser. The music is pop with R&B underneath — character-driven lyrics with specific details and chronological arcs.

Wordmark Rules

Always Dela Gothic One. Always uppercase. Letter-spacing -0.03em to -0.05em. Clear space = cap height of the "C." Never stretch, skew, outline, shadow, or gradient. The font does the heavy lifting.

Approved Lockups

Soot on Cherry is the hero — tentpole moments, single covers, merch. Cherry on Soot and Cream on Cherry are alternates. Soot on Pink and Pink on Soot for social. Soot on Cream for press. Icy Blue on Soot for playlist covers and digital highlights.
