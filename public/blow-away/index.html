Director's Treatment — Caitlin Byk

Blow Away

Dir. Brendan Lynch
The Varied
A Note

Yo! Thanks for considering me for this. I love this song. I've been rinsing it ever since you guys dropped it. I loved running around with Vince on the Venice boardwalk capturing the social content for this song... for me, this story feels like an extension of that.

The short version is this: we shoot Caitlin's world on digital with raw 35mm texture woven throughout, we let the song's energy drive the visuals, and we tell a story about a romance that fades with the summer................... gay!

01 — The Feel

Caitlin watches her skater boyfriend walk into Venice skate park like a local king and she's glowing. The whole video lives in that energy — fun, bouncing, sun-drenched. We shoot digital with handheld 35mm texture cut throughout — messy, grainy, real life snippets that make the early stuff feel like memory.

It's not a sad video. It's a fun video that turns out to be a goodbye.

I think of it in three acts. Act one is Venice — the golden world, his world, the party. Act two is the valley — the empty pool, the quiet, the moment she sees him clearly. Act three is what happens after the video ends. We never show it.

02 — The World

Venice, unfiltered. Cracked sidewalks, tagged walls, weird boardwalk characters. VS The abandoned valley house with a drained swimming pool.

Hard midday sun early, golden hour in the middle, night for the pool. Digital is the backbone. The 35mm is texture — handheld snippets of Venice that we cut in like polaroids. I want the two locations to feel like two different movies, with Cailtin as the throughline.

We don't *have* to shoot at Venice skatepark... but it would be cool. There are other parks around SoCal that may offer us more flexibility from a production standpoint. The skaters should be legit though. We need to sell that part. We have some lines to tap for real life skaters, so I'm not worried.

03 — Caitlin

Back to Black-era Amy meets Priscilla. Fun, a little messy, the most magnetic person in every frame — giving all that energy to a boy who doesn't fully see it. She laughs too loud, sits on things you're not supposed to sit on, gets pouty when he tries to leave. She's having the best day of her life and she's not being precious about it.

By the end she's not heartbroken... just more herself. She learns a bit and stops attaching all the fun to him.

Wardrobe: normal. Tank top, shorts, sneakers or barefoot. If it looks like a stylist picked it, it's wrong. I'd honestly rather she show up in whatever she was wearing that morning.

04 — The Arc

She starts watching him get greeted like a celebrity and loving it. By the night pool she's seeing him clearly for the first time. The shift happens behind her eyes. You don't catch it until it's already done.

Ninety percent of the video is still golden and fun. The last ten percent just breathes differently. The video ends right at the edge of act three — she's alone, she's still, and whatever comes next is hers. We cut to black before she gets there. NONE of this story / acting should feel heavy handed. The visual language of this sun-drenched skatepark / empty swimming pool will do a lot of the heavy lifting in terms of vibe.

05 — Camera & Film

We stay mostly digital. The whole narrative lives there.

35mm as texture layer — i want someone with a film camera grabbing handheld snippets throughout the day. Venice weirdness, candid moments, the boardwalk, skaters, Caitlin laughing between takes. We roll maybe 5-8 minutes of film total. just raw, gritty, real-life stuff that we cut into the digital like found footage. Messy and beautiful.

The film shows up early and often in the Venice sections — mixed into the digital, not replacing it. By the time we get to the valley, it's gone. If we make this transition intentional, that one last flash of 35mm at the end is gonna hit.

Slow motion for the bouquet spill, kickflips, hair whipping on the boardwalk. Only in moments when it makes sense.

06 — Performance

Three setups at the Venice bowl: standing wide (skaters dropping in behind her), sitting on the lip (intimate, to camera), and the plexiglass shot (super wide, caitlin standing on plexiglass singing down into lens). Syd on set for all performance — intention and confidence, not pop choreo. She moves like herself, just dialed in. If it ever looks like she's "doing choreo," we fucked up.

The plexiglass recurs three times. I think these three shots might end up being the spine of the whole edit.

07 — Key Moments
The Arrival
He walks in, the park rearranges around him, she watches from one step outside the circle. I want to live on her face here longer than feels comfortable.
The Bouquet
He gives her an absurdly huge bouquet. Like, comically large. She can barely see over it. I'm feeling sun flowers. Later the flowers spill and scatter in slow motion. They blow away. We never pick them up. (this is the money shot)
The Smoke Transition
smoking at the edge of the bowl. Camera tracks the smoke up, spins in the sky, comes down on Caitlin driving to the valley for Verse 2.
He Leaves
She pulls his arm, he goes anyway. First time her expression isn't fun. This is the shot I'm most worried about getting right. No overacting!!
The 35mm Flash
End of the video. The grain floods back. Venice, golden hour, the good times. Warm and beautiful and over. Not quite a sucker punch, but i want us to feel the change.
08 — Night Pool

The empty pool at night is where the video lands. MAYBE we pull in the projector casting color on the concrete walls — cyan, magenta, amber. Caitlin finishes the song here alone. Still vibing but solo.

The energy is the difference between dancing at a party and dancing in your room. She's not sad in here. She's just alone for the first time and okay with it. Slightly more introspection from Caitlin.

09 — Final Image

The 35mm flash floods back — Venice, golden, the whole circus. I love the idea of her standing on a skateboard being (slowly and safely) pulled down the boardwalk by some crazy shirtless Venetian. Just grinning. Not cool, not posed — actually having fun. That image on film with the boardwalk blowing past behind her is going to feel like a memory even while you're watching it. Then it's gone. RIP.

Back to digital. The empty pool. Night. Caitlin sitting alone. Knees up. Not watching anyone. Not performing. Just sitting. Maybe she kept his board? Maybe its on its side nearby, wheels barely spinning?

Black.

Not an ending in my mind. Act three starts here but we don't see it. To me the feeling is the moment where Pricilla realizes shes her own woman - not Elvis' wife.

10 — Tone

Fun. Not sullen. Raw. Not try-hard.

The song bounces and the video should bounce with it. Even in the heavier moments, there's warmth. A skater bails in the background. The bouquet is too big to carry. Someone's dog wanders into frame. The fun is real — that's what makes the goodbye land. If the video isn't a good time to watch, we've absolutely fucked it.

She starts proud to be next to him. She ends sitting still for the first time. The whole thing makes you want to move.

Shot-by-Shot Storyline
0:00–0:06 Cold Open 35mm
Morning Venice. Empty boardwalk, long shadows, concrete still cool. Tagged walls, palm trees, the ocean flat and silver. The city before anyone's in it. This is where the 35mm texture lives heaviest — grainy, handheld, the city before it's awake. I want it to feel like we got there too early. Beat drops at 0:06.
0:06–0:20 The Arrival 35mm
Venice alive. Hero Skater pulls up to the park. Friends greet him — hands slapped, board tossed. We shoot through the group toward him. Camera keeps drifting to Caitlin watching from one step outside. She's glowing. Someone's already eating shit on a ledge behind her. We hang on her face — pride, admiration, full buy-in. Film snippets cut in throughout — the boardwalk, the skaters, the chaos.
0:20–0:30 "17, klepto queen" 35mm
Caitlin performing in front of the bowl. Wide angle, skaters dropping in behind her. Syd choreo here. Intercut b-roll: hand grabbing a bottle, fake ID getting flashed (playful, not literal). The b-roll should feel like stuff we caught, not stuff we staged (v important). A skater nearly clips her mid-performance and she doesn't flinch — or she does, and we keep it.
0:30–0:38 "23, friday night degree" 35mm
Friend fake-proposes to Caitlin (mocking Hero Skater). She gasps, plays into it. Hero Skater shoves him with the board, laughing. Everyone's cracking up. Cut to them sitting on edge of bowl. Boys drinking. She leans into him. He watches the session. Quick detail: CZ ring flash. I want to give them room to improv here — the funniest version of this is probably all the shit that isn't planned.
0:38–0:50 "They'd go all night long" 35mm → digital
Caitlin performance for the ashtray line — edge of bowl, tighter framing. Big skateboard transition (board drops into bowl, camera rides the momentum). Bouquet presentation — Hero Skater gives her the massive flowers. She holds them to her chest. Can barely see over them. The bouquet needs to be embarrassingly big. That's what makes it work later when it all falls apart.
0:50–1:15 Chorus 1 — "She don't wanna go" Film/digital transition
Intercut three performance setups: wide bowl, edge sitting, plexiglass #1 (midday, half-smile, teasing). Skaters spilling out messy and laughing, bouquet getting jostled. Someone tries to smell the flowers and she swats them away. Jay at edge of bowl, smoke blown up, camera tracks it skyward, spins, comes down on... The 35mm texture starts thinning out through the chorus. By the time we hit the valley it's gone.
1:15–1:25 "34, uni are at the door" Digital
Caitlin driving. Hero Skater in passenger seat. He's the one who wanted to come out here — heard about some abandoned house in the valley with a drained pool, a perfect bowl. He's navigating on his phone, barely looking up. She's just the ride. She sings casually, windows down. Glances at him — he doesn't look back. Camera catches it. Overgrown hedges, dead lawns, sun-bleached FOR SALE signs. I think the less we do here the better. Just two people in a car and the silence between them.
1:25–1:35 "41, never had a son" Digital
They pull up to the abandoned house. This is his mission, not hers. He's already out of the car. She follows him through the side yard, camera following like it shouldn't be here. They reach the back and there it is — the empty pool.
1:35–1:50 Pre-Chorus 2 — "They'd go all night long" Digital
Hero Skater drops into the pool. Sick. Caitlin at the edge jumping up and down, clapping. She's genuinely stoked — like a kid watching fireworks. They sit together, sun going down. She watches him skate. Plexiglass #2 — golden hour, eyes closed, softer. She's starting to drift. Smoke curling through pool light.
1:50–2:10 Chorus 2 — "She don't wanna go" Digital
The turn. He wants to leave. She pulls his arm — playful, then real. He goes anyway. She watches him walk through the house. First time her expression isn't fun. She's alone at the house. Sun fading. Quick flashes of Venice bowl, shorter and more fragmented. Bouquet spills — petals scatter across pool deck in slow motion. They blow away.
2:10–2:35 Chorus 3 / Outro — "Blowin' away" Digital → 35mm → Digital
Night pool. Projector light on the walls. Caitlin finishes the song alone. Still vibing, solo energy. Plexiglass #3 — night, projector colors on her face, expression changed. Not sad. Awake. "Blowin' away" — the 35mm flash. One last burst. The grain floods back. Sun-drenched Venice. The boardwalk. Caitlin on a skateboard being pulled slowly down the boardwalk by a crazy shirtless Venetian. Just grinning. Not cool, not posed — actually having fun. That image on film with the boardwalk blowing past behind her is going to feel like a memory even while you're watching it. It's warm and beautiful and the world we started in — but we've been in the clean digital long enough that the film stock now feels like it belongs to a different day. The warmth comes flooding back and then it's gone.
2:35–2:40 Final Image Digital
The empty pool. Night. Projector light has gone soft. Colors bleeding across concrete. Caitlin sitting on the curved floor of the pool, alone. Knees up, arms wrapped around them. Not watching anyone. Not performing. Just sitting. Black.
