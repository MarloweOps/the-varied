Director's Treatment — Caitlin Byk

Blow Away

Dir. Brendan Lynch
The Varied
A Note

Hey — thank you for considering me for this. I love this song. I've been rinsing it ever since you guys dropped it. It's been fun to shoot the short form content for it and now put together a full music video treatment.

The lyrics are doing something really smart — telling a decades-long story inside a song that makes you want to move — and I think the video has to honor both of those things at once.

What follows is the visual world, the arc, the camera philosophy, and a beat-by-beat storyline mapped to the song. It's detailed because I believe in the idea, but the short version is this: we shoot Caitlin's world on 35mm and digital, we let the song's energy drive the visuals, and we tell a story that feels like a summer you only realize was the last one after it's over.

Let's make something great.

— Brendan

01 — The Feel

Caitlin watches her skater boyfriend walk into Venice skate park like a local king and she's glowing. The whole video lives in that energy — fun, bouncing, sun-drenched. We shoot on 35mm early (the spell) and migrate to digital as her eyes open. One last flash of film at the end hits like nostalgia for something that happened twenty minutes ago.

It's not a sad video. It's a fun video that turns out to be a goodbye.

02 — The World

Venice, unfiltered. Cracked sidewalks, tagged walls, weird boardwalk characters, palm trees dead at the tips. Then an abandoned valley house with a drained swimming pool.

The light tracks Caitlin's arc — hard midday sun early, golden hour in the middle, night for the pool. 35mm makes the early stuff glow. Digital makes the later stuff clear. Anamorphic 2.39:1 the whole way through.

03 — Caitlin

Herself with the volume up.

Back to Black-era Amy meets Priscilla. She's fun, a little messy, the most magnetic person in every frame — giving all that energy to a boy who doesn't fully see it. She's not a sad girl or a pop star. She laughs too loud, sits on things you're not supposed to sit on, gets pouty when he tries to leave.

By the end she's not broken — just present. The fun didn't leave her. She just stopped attaching it to him.

Wardrobe: normal. Tank top, shorts, sneakers or barefoot. If it looks like a stylist picked it, it's wrong.

04 — The Arc

No plot. A five-degree turn.

She starts watching him get greeted like a celebrity and loving it. By the night pool she's seeing him clearly for the first time. The shift happens behind her eyes — never announced, never performed.

Ninety percent of the video is still golden and fun. The last ten percent just breathes differently. The audience should feel like they watched something joyful and only catch the goodbye on the second watch.

05 — Camera & Film

Two cameras, one migration.

35mm opens (Kodak Vision3 — the warmth, the grain, the spell). Digital takes over as she wakes up (Alexa Mini LF — precision, clarity). By the night pool we're fully digital. Then one last flash of 35mm at the end — sun-drenched Venice, full grain — and it devastates because the film stock now feels like it's from another time.

Both formats anamorphic. 48fps overcranking for the bouquet spill, kickflips, hair whipping on the boardwalk. Slow motion as punctuation.

06 — Performance

Caitlin performs at the Venice bowl in three setups: standing wide (skaters dropping in behind her), sitting on the lip (intimate, to camera), and the plexiglass shot from beneath the pool. Syd (choreographer) on set for all performance — the goal is intention and confidence, not pop choreo. She moves like herself, just dialed in.

The plexiglass recurs three times — midday (teasing), golden hour (drifting), night with projector light (awake). Same composition, different Caitlin. It's a time device and an emotional tracker.

07 — Key Moments
The Arrival
He walks in, the park rearranges around him, she watches from one step outside the circle. Our emotional baseline.
The Bouquet
He gives her an absurdly huge bouquet. Later the flowers spill and scatter in slow motion. They blow away. We never pick them up.
The Smoke Transition
Jay at the edge of the bowl. Camera tracks the smoke up, spins in the sky, comes down on Caitlin driving to the valley.
He Leaves
She pulls his arm, he goes anyway. First time her expression isn't fun.
The 35mm Flash
End of the video. The grain floods back. Venice, golden hour, the good times. Warm and beautiful and unmistakably over.
08 — Night Pool

The empty pool at night is where the video lands. Fully digital. Projector casting color on the concrete walls — cyan, magenta, amber. Caitlin finishes the song here alone. Still vibing but solo.

The energy is the difference between dancing at a party and dancing in your room. Both are real. One just knows something the other doesn't.

09 — Final Image

One breath too long.

Flash of 35mm — Venice, golden, the world we started in. Then back to digital. Night. The empty pool. Caitlin sitting alone. Knees up. Not watching anyone. His board on its side nearby, wheels barely spinning.

Camera rises above her. She gets smaller. She doesn't look up. Wheels stop. Clean digital holds one breath too long.

Black.

10 — Tone

Fun. Not sullen. Raw. Not try-hard.

True to SoCal and true to Caitlin. She starts proud to be next to him. She ends sitting still for the first time. The whole thing makes you want to move.

And somewhere underneath all that movement, you feel her stop.

Shot-by-Shot Storyline
0:00–0:06 Cold Open 35mm
Morning Venice. Empty boardwalk, long shadows, concrete still cool. Texture shots — tagged walls, palm trees, the ocean flat and silver. The city before anyone's in it. Beat drops at 0:06.
0:06–0:20 The Arrival 35mm
Venice alive. Hero Skater pulls up to the park. Friends greet him — hands slapped, board tossed. We shoot through the group toward him. Camera keeps drifting to Caitlin watching from one step outside. She's glowing. Group disperses. We hang on her face — pride, admiration, full buy-in.
0:20–0:30 "17, klepto queen" 35mm
Caitlin performing in front of the bowl. Wide angle, skaters dropping in behind her. She plays to camera, confident and cool. Syd choreo here. Intercut b-roll: hand grabbing a bottle, fake ID getting flashed (playful, not literal).
0:30–0:38 "23, friday night degree" 35mm
Back to narrative. Friend fake-proposes to Caitlin (mocking Hero Skater). She gasps, plays into it. Hero Skater shoves him with the board, laughing. Cut to Caitlin and Hero Skater sitting on edge of bowl. Boys drinking. She leans into him. He watches the session. Quick detail: CZ ring flash.
0:38–0:50 "They'd go all night long" 35mm → digital
Cut to Caitlin performance for the ashtray line — edge of bowl, tighter framing. Big skateboard transition (board drops into bowl, camera rides the momentum). Bouquet presentation — Hero Skater gives her the massive flowers. She holds them to her chest.
0:50–1:15 Chorus 1 — "She don't wanna go" Film/digital transition
Intercut three performance setups: wide bowl, edge sitting, plexiglass #1 (midday, half-smile, teasing). Narrative intercuts: skaters spilling out messy and laughing, bouquet getting jostled. "Smoking all the time" — jay at edge of bowl, smoke blown up, camera tracks it skyward, spins, comes down on...
1:15–1:25 "34, uni are at the door" Digital
Caitlin driving. Hero Skater in passenger seat. Windows down, valley streets. She sings casually. He's on his phone. She glances at him — he doesn't look back. Camera catches it. Detail shots: overgrown hedges, dead lawns, sun-bleached FOR SALE signs.
1:25–1:35 "41, never had a son" Digital
They pull up to the abandoned house. The break-in — through the fence, around the side, camera following like it shouldn't be here. Push through dark hallway. Discover the empty pool. Hold on it. Caitlin climbs down, exploring.
1:35–1:50 Pre-Chorus 2 — "They'd go all night long" Digital
Hero Skater drops into the pool. Raw and loud. Caitlin at the edge jumping up and down, clapping. They sit together, sun going down. She watches him skate. Plexiglass #2 — golden hour, eyes closed, softer. She's starting to drift. Smoke curling through pool light.
1:50–2:10 Chorus 2 — "She don't wanna go" Digital
The turn. He wants to leave. She pulls his arm — playful, then real. He goes anyway. She watches him walk through the house. First time her expression isn't fun. She's alone at the house. Sun fading. Performance callbacks — quick flashes of Venice bowl, shorter and more fragmented. Bouquet spills — petals scatter across pool deck in slow motion. They blow away.
2:10–2:35 Chorus 3 / Outro — "Blowin' away" Digital → 35mm → Digital
Night pool. Projector light on the walls. Caitlin finishes the song alone. Still vibing, solo energy. Plexiglass #3 — night, projector colors on her face, expression changed. Not sad. Awake. "Blowin' away" — 35mm flash: Venice, skate park, golden hour, the good times flooding back in warm grain. Beautiful and unmistakably over. Back to digital.
2:35–2:40 Final Image Digital
Empty pool. Night. Caitlin sitting alone. His board on its side, wheels spinning, slowing. Camera rises above her. Pool widens. She gets smaller. Doesn't look up. Wheels stop. Clean image holds. Black.
