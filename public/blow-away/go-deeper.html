Treatment Blow Away — Go Deeper

Director's Extended Notes

Go Deeper.

Blow Away · Caitlin Byk · Dir. Brendan Lynch
Runtime: 2:40 · Format: 35mm → Alexa Mini LF · Anamorphic 2.39:1
Locations: Venice Skate Park · Venice Boardwalk · Abandoned Valley House · Car
Character Direction

Caitlin.

Back to Black-era Amy Winehouse meets Priscilla (Sofia Coppola version)

Caitlin is herself with the volume turned up slightly. Not a character — just a sharper version of who she already is. The girl who'd actually follow a skater boy into an abandoned house at midnight. The girl who smokes his jay and gets pouty when he wants to leave. She's fun, a little messy, the most magnetic person in every frame — and she's giving all that energy to a boy who doesn't fully see it.

The Priscilla side: She starts the video inside someone else's world and she genuinely loves it there. She's the passenger who's more interesting than the driver, but she has no idea. Her gaze shifts across the piece — worship early, observation in the middle, quiet recognition by the end. That shift is never performed or announced. It happens behind her eyes. The camera catches it. She doesn't.

The Back to Black side: Amy wasn't a victim. She was fun and reckless and clearly the most alive person in the room, and she chose to orbit someone who didn't match her. She knew and didn't care — or she cared and stayed anyway, which is worse and more honest. Caitlin carries that same energy. There's a slight recklessness to her — not self-destruction, just that nineteen-year-old thing where you're one beer past caring and everything is either the best night of your life or the worst.

She is NOT
  • A sad girl. Not moping. Not waiting around looking wounded.
  • A music video pop star. No posing, no "serving looks," no choreo that feels like a reel.
  • A blank canvas. She's specific. She has opinions. She has a temper. She laughs too loud.
She IS
  • The most interesting person at the skate park and the last one to know it.
  • Genuinely happy in the beginning. Not faking it. The worship is real.
  • Playful, physical, a little chaotic. She leans into people. Grabs arms.
  • By the end: still. Not broken — just clear.
The important thing: She doesn't become sad. She becomes present. The fun doesn't drain out of her — it stops being attached to someone else. The final pool scene isn't grief. It's the first time she's alone with herself, and she's fine. That's the Priscilla landing. Not heartbreak. Sovereignty.

Wardrobe: Normal. High school girl who raided her own closet. Tank top, shorts, maybe a flannel tied at the waist. Sneakers or barefoot. If it looks like a stylist picked it, it's wrong. The Back to Black edge comes from her energy, not her clothes.
Choreographer note (Syd): On set for all bowl performance setups. The goal isn't choreography in a pop sense — it's helping Caitlin move with intention and confidence so every performance beat feels mature and natural, not stiff or self-conscious.
Storyline — Beat by Beat

The arc in motion.

0:00–0:06 Cold Open 35mm

Morning Venice. The world before anyone's awake in it. Empty boardwalk, long shadows, the concrete still cool. A skater rolls through frame in the distance. A palm tree catches early light. The ocean is flat and silver.

Quick cuts, all texture: tagged walls, a bicycle chained to nothing, a coffee cup on the edge of a bench, the Venice sign catching sun. The track hasn't kicked in yet. We're breathing.

At 0:06, the beat drops and we snap into motion.

0:06–0:20 The Arrival 35mm

Venice fully alive. The skate park is buzzing. We're handheld, moving through the crowd, matching the energy of the track's opening bounce.

Hero Skater pulls up. He walks into the park and it rearranges around him. We shoot through the group of friends that greet him — hands slapped, someone tosses him a board, the easy magnetism of a guy who owns this space. The camera finds him through the crowd the way everyone finds him.

But it keeps drifting to Caitlin. She's right there, one step outside the circle, watching the way the boys treat him like a local king. She's glowing. Not performing it — genuinely, honestly proud to be next to this.

We hang on Caitlin for a beat. The camera holds on her face — the admiration, the pride, the full-body buy-in. This is our emotional baseline. Everything we see from here forward, we measure against this look on her face.

0:20–0:30 Verse 1A — "17, klepto queen" 35mm
17, klepto queen
Met him with a bottle and a fake ID

Caitlin performing in front of the bowl. Wide angle. Skaters dropping in behind her, carving the bowl as she sings. She's playing to camera — confident, cool, owning the space. The contrast between her and the chaos behind her is the frame. She's still, they're in motion. She's the center of gravity.

B-roll intercuts: close-up of a hand grabbing a bottle, a fake ID getting flashed (playful, not serious). Details that nod to the lyrics without being literal.

0:30–0:38 Verse 1B — "23, friday night degree" 35mm
23, friday night degree
He's on one knee with a CZ ring

Back to narrative. The fake proposal: One of Hero Skater's friends drops to one knee in front of Caitlin — mocking Hero Skater. Caitlin plays into it, gasps dramatically. Hero Skater playfully shoves the friend with his board. It's pure friend-group energy. Genuine and chaotic.

Cut to: Caitlin and Hero Skater sitting on the edge of the bowl, legs dangling. She leans into him. He watches the session. She's watching him watch the session. The camera catches this — her looking at him while he looks elsewhere. The first quiet hint of the dynamic.

Detail shot: Flash of the CZ ring on her finger. Light catches it for a frame. We don't linger.

0:38–0:50 Pre-Chorus — "They'd go all night long" 35mm thinning to digital
They'd go all night long / riding the rising sun seeing their life slip away
Now they're night-swimming laps in the ashtray / And they're sleeping all day

Caitlin at the edge of the bowl. Tighter framing. She sings the ashtray line and the camera catches smoke — a cigarette, a jay — curling into the golden-hour light behind her.

Big skateboard transition: A skater drops into the bowl and the camera follows the board down the curve — we ride the momentum of the drop and use it as a wipe/transition into:

The Bouquet. Hero Skater appears with this absurdly massive bouquet of flowers. Way too big. He presents it to Caitlin with full dumb confidence. She takes it, holds it against her chest like a trophy, laughing. It fills the anamorphic frame with color. For a beat, they're the most romantic thing in Venice.

0:50–1:15 Chorus 1 — "She don't wanna go" 35mm → digital transition
She don't wanna go / He don't wanna stay
Drinking all the time / Hairs are turning gray
She just wanna talk / He don't got nothin' to say
Smoking all the time / till they blow away

The chorus opens up. Intercut between three performance setups:

Perf A Standing in front of bowl — wide, full body. Skaters behind her.
Perf B Sitting on edge of bowl — intimate, handheld, singing directly to camera.
Perf C — Plexiglass #1 First plexiglass hit. Caitlin slides down the curve and lands over the lens. We're beneath her, looking up. Hair falling, midday sky behind her. Half-smile, teasing. She's fully in the spell.

"Smoking all the time / till they blow away" — The Smoke Transition: Camera tracks the smoke upward, keeps going, spins in the sky — the world rotates — and comes back down to land on:

1:15–1:25 Verse 2A — "34, uni are at the door" Digital — Alexa Mini LF
34, uni are at the door
Black eye blues / Orange bottles on the floor

Caitlin driving. Windows down. Hero Skater in the passenger seat. Valley streets — residential, dry hills, flat SoCal sprawl that looks nothing like Venice. The grain is gone. The image is sharper, cleaner. The audience may not clock it yet, but they feel it.

Caitlin sings to the windshield, to the road. He's looking at his phone or out the window. She glances at him. He doesn't look back. A small, almost invisible beat — but the camera catches it.

Detail shots as they drive: overgrown hedges, a sun-bleached FOR SALE sign, a chain-link fence around a dead lawn. The valley in decay.

1:25–1:35 Verse 2B — "41, never had a son" Digital
41, never had a son
One last chance / She's too busy having fun

They pull up to the abandoned valley house. Sun-beaten, dead yard, a real estate sign face-down in the weeds.

The break-in. The camera gets sneaky. We follow them through a gap in the fence, around the side. Push through a dark hallway, past peeling wallpaper and dusty light, and discover:

The empty swimming pool. Drained, curved, cracked concrete. A perfect bowl. The camera holds on it for a beat — this void in the middle of someone's abandoned backyard. Beautiful and desolate at the same time.

"She's too busy having fun" — Caitlin is already climbing down into the pool, exploring, touching the walls. She's not thinking about what this place means. She's just here.

1:35–1:50 Pre-Chorus 2 Digital
They'd go all night long / Riding the rising sun / Seeing their life slip away
Now they're night-swimming laps in the ashtray / And they're sleeping all day

Hero Skater drops into the empty pool. He carves the walls. Raw and loud — board on cracked concrete, the sound echoing off the empty house. Wide and low, the god-angle. Same heroic framing as Venice bowl, but now in this abandoned, private space.

Caitlin jumps up and down at the edge of the pool, clapping, loving it. This is their adventure. Their secret. For a moment the energy is back to full — fun, reckless, alive.

They sit together at the edge of the pool. The sun is going down — golden hour in the valley. She leans into him. He keeps watching the pool like he wants to go back in.

Plexiglass #2 — Golden hour. She's standing over the lens, eyes closed, singing softly. Anamorphic flare streaks the glass. Warmer than the first, but something is different. She's starting to drift.

1:50–2:10 Chorus 2 — The Turn Digital
She don't wanna go / He don't wanna stay
Drinking all the time / Hairs are turning gray
She just wanna talk / He don't got nothin' to say
Smoking all the time / till they blow away

"She don't wanna go / He don't wanna stay" — literal. He wants to leave. She pulls his arm, tries to make him stay. Playful at first — pouty, mock-tantrumy. But there's an edge to it. She's clinging and he's already halfway gone.

He leaves her. Not dramatically. He just... goes. Grabs his board, heads back through the house. She watches him walk away. The camera holds on her face — and it's the first time in the video her expression isn't fun.

She's alone at the house. Sun fading. Standing at the edge of the empty pool, looking down into it.

Bouquet payoff: The bouquet finally spills. Petals scatter across the dry pool deck. Wind catches them. Slow motion. They drift across the empty pool. They blow away. We never see them picked up.

2:10–2:35 Chorus 3 / Outro — "Blowin' away" Digital → 35mm flash → Digital
Blowin' away / Blowin' away / Blowin' away

Night. The projector is on — casting color across the curved concrete walls. Cyan, magenta, warm amber. Caitlin finishes the song alone in the empty pool. Still vibing. The track still bounces. But the energy is solo now. The difference between dancing at a party and dancing alone in your room. Both are real. One just knows something the other doesn't.

Plexiglass #3 — Night. Projector light throwing color across her face from below. She looks down into the lens and her expression has changed. Not sad. Not angry. Clear. This is the Priscilla moment. She's not in anyone else's world anymore.

"Blowin' away" — The 35mm flash. One last burst. The grain floods back. Sun-drenched Venice. The skate park. Hero Skater pulling up, friends greeting him. Caitlin glowing by his side. The whole golden circus at full volume. It's warm and beautiful and unmistakably the world we started in — but we've been in the clean digital long enough that the film stock now feels like it's from another time. It's not the present. It's the echo. It's the goodbye. The warmth comes flooding back and then it's gone.

2:35–2:40 Final Image Digital

The empty pool. Night. Digital.

Caitlin is sitting on the curved floor of the pool, alone. Knees up, arms wrapped around them. Not watching anyone. Not performing. Just sitting.

Hero Skater's board is on its side nearby. Wheels still spinning. Barely.

The camera is above her, looking straight down. It begins to rise. The pool widens. She gets smaller. The projector light makes the concrete look like the surface of another planet.

She doesn't look up. The wheels stop spinning. The clean digital image holds for one breath too long.

Cut to black.

Caitlin arrives at Venice in the glow of her skater boyfriend's world. She watches him get greeted like a king and loves every second of it. She performs at the bowl, hangs with the crew, plays into their chaos. She's a passenger on his ride and she's thriving. They leave Venice — she drives them to an abandoned valley house where he skates the empty pool. She claps, she cheers, she's still all in. But when the sun goes down and he wants to leave, she clings — and he goes anyway. She's alone in the empty pool as night falls. She finishes the song there, bathed in projector light, still vibing but solo now. One last flash of the golden Venice days hits like a wave — warm, grainy, beautiful, and unmistakably over. Then we're back in the pool. Digital. Clear. She's sitting alone. The board stops spinning. She doesn't look up. Cut to black.
