Blow Away — Extended Notes

Storyline & Shot List

Caitlin Byk · Dir. Brendan Lynch
Runtime: 2:40 · 35mm → Alexa Mini LF · Anamorphic 2.39:1
Venice Skate Park · Venice Boardwalk · Abandoned Valley House · Car
Three Acts

Act one is Venice — the golden world, his world, the party. Everything is fun and loud and 35mm and she's having the best day of her life next to a boy who barely notices.

Act two is the valley — the empty pool, the quiet, the moment she sees him clearly for the first time. He leaves. She stays.

Act three is what happens after the video ends. We never show it. We just get her to the door.

The final image isn't a sad ending — it's a threshold. Whatever comes next is hers. The song is fun. The video needs to be fun too. Even the heavier beats should have warmth and energy. A skater eats shit in the background. The bouquet is too big to carry. Someone's dog wanders into frame. The goodbye only works if the good time was real.

Character Direction — Caitlin

Back to Black-era Amy meets Priscilla.

Not a character — just a sharper version of who she already is. The girl who'd actually follow a skater boy into an abandoned house at midnight. The girl who smokes his jay and gets pouty when he wants to leave. Fun, a little messy, the most magnetic person in every frame — giving all that energy to a boy who doesn't fully see it. She's having the best day of her life and she's not being precious about it.

The Priscilla side: She starts the video inside someone else's world and she genuinely loves it there. She's the passenger who's more interesting than the driver, but she has no idea. Her gaze shifts across the piece — worship early, observation in the middle, quiet recognition by the end. It happens behind her eyes. The camera catches it. She doesn't.

The Back to Black side: Amy wasn't a victim. She was fun and reckless and clearly the most alive person in the room, and she chose to orbit someone who didn't match her. She knew and didn't care — or she cared and stayed anyway, which is worse and more honest. Caitlin carries that same energy. She claps when he drops into the pool. She gasps at the fake proposal. She pulls his arm when he tries to leave. There's a slight recklessness to her — not self-destruction, just that nineteen-year-old thing where you're one beer past caring and everything is either the best night of your life or the worst.

She is NOT
  • A sad girl. Not moping. Not waiting around looking wounded.
  • A music video pop star. No posing, no "serving looks," no choreo that feels like a performance reel.
  • A blank canvas. She's specific. She has opinions. She has a temper. She laughs too loud.
She IS
  • The most interesting person at the skate park and the last one to know it.
  • Genuinely happy in the beginning. Not faking it. The worship is real — that's what makes the shift matter.
  • Playful, physical, a little chaotic. She leans into people. Grabs arms. Sits on things you're not supposed to sit on.
  • Funny. She's actually funny. The video should let her be funny.
  • By the end: still. Not broken — just clear. The fun is still hers. She's just taking it with her.
The important thing: She doesn't become sad. She becomes present. The fun doesn't drain out of her — it stops being attached to someone else. The final pool scene isn't grief. It's the first time she's alone with herself, and she's fine. Act three starts here. We just don't get to see it.
Wardrobe: Normal. High school girl who raided her own closet. Tank top, shorts, maybe a flannel tied at the waist. Sneakers or barefoot. If it looks like a stylist picked it, it's wrong. I'd honestly rather she show up in whatever she was wearing that morning than have us overthink this.

Choreographer (Syd): On set for all bowl performance setups. Not choreography in a pop sense — helping her move with intention so every performance beat feels natural, not stiff or self-conscious. I really want to avoid any moment where it feels like she's "doing choreo." It should look like she just moves like that.
Storyline — Beat by Beat
0:00–0:06 Cold Open 35mm

Morning Venice. The world before anyone's awake in it. Empty boardwalk, long shadows, the concrete still cool. A skater rolls through frame in the distance. A palm tree catches early light. The ocean is flat and silver. A guy is asleep on a bench with a hat over his face. A seagull is eating someone's leftover fries.

Quick cuts, all texture: tagged walls, a bicycle chained to nothing, a coffee cup on the edge of a bench, the Venice sign catching sun. The track hasn't kicked in yet. We're breathing. I want this to feel like we got there too early. Like we're killing time before the rest of the video shows up. At 0:06, the beat drops and we snap into motion.

0:06–0:20 The Arrival 35mm

Venice fully alive. Hero Skater pulls up to the park and it rearranges around him. We shoot through the group of friends that greet him — hands slapped, someone tosses him a board. Someone's already eating shit on a ledge in the background. Nobody cares.

But it keeps drifting to Caitlin. She's right there, one step outside the circle, watching the way the boys treat him like a local king. She's glowing. Not performing it — genuinely proud to be next to this. We hang on Caitlin for a beat. The camera holds on her face — the admiration, the pride, the full buy-in. I want to live on her face here longer than feels comfortable. We need this look to land because everything later measures against it.

0:20–0:30 "17, klepto queen" 35mm
17, klepto queen / Met him with a bottle and a fake ID

Caitlin performing in front of the bowl. Wide angle, skaters dropping in behind her. She's playing to camera. Syd's work shows here — she moves with intention. Not dancing, not standing rigid. Something in between that feels like her.

B-roll intercuts: hand grabbing a bottle, a fake ID getting flashed (playful, not serious). I don't want to be too literal with the lyrics here. The b-roll should feel like stuff we caught, not stuff we staged. A skater nearly clips her mid-performance and she doesn't flinch — or she does, and we keep it. Either way it's gold.

0:30–0:38 "23, friday night degree" 35mm
23, friday night degree / He's on one knee with a CZ ring

Caitlin hanging with the skaters around the bowl. Loose, fun, messy. This should feel like a hangout, not a scene. The fake proposal: one of Hero Skater's friends drops to one knee. She plays into it, gasps dramatically. Hero Skater shoves the friend with his board, laughing. Everyone's cracking up. I want to let this breathe and see if we get something real out of it. Give them room to improv. The funniest version of this is probably one we don't plan.

Cut to: Caitlin and Hero Skater sitting on the edge of the bowl, legs dangling. She leans into him. He's watching the session. She's watching him watch the session — the camera catches it. The first quiet hint of the dynamic. Detail shot: CZ ring on her finger. Don't linger.

0:38–0:50 Pre-Chorus — "They'd go all night long" 35mm thinning to digital
They'd go all night long / riding the rising sun seeing their life slip away
Now they're night-swimming laps in the ashtray / And they're sleeping all day

Caitlin at the edge of the bowl now, different angle, tighter. She sings the ashtray line and the camera catches smoke curling into the golden-hour light behind her.

Big skateboard transition: a skater drops into the bowl, camera follows the board down the curve — we ride the momentum into: The Bouquet. Hero Skater appears with this absurdly massive bouquet. Way too big. She takes it, holds it against her chest like a trophy, laughing. She can barely see over it. I'm obsessed with this image. The bouquet needs to be comically large — the kind of thing you can barely carry. That's what makes it work later when it all falls apart.

0:50–1:15 Chorus 1 — "She don't wanna go" 35mm → digital transition
She don't wanna go / He don't wanna stay / Drinking all the time / Hairs are turning gray
She just wanna talk / He don't got nothin' to say / Smoking all the time / till they blow away

Intercut three performance setups: standing wide at bowl, sitting on edge, and Plexiglass #1 — Caitlin slides down the curve and lands over the lens. We're beneath her, looking up. Hair falling, midday sky. Half-smile, teasing. She's fully in the spell.

"Smoking all the time" — The Smoke Transition: camera tracks smoke upward, keeps going, spins in the sky, comes back down to land on...

1:15–1:25 "34, uni are at the door" Digital — Alexa Mini LF
34, uni are at the door / Black eye blues / Orange bottles on the floor

Caitlin driving. Windows down. Hero Skater in the passenger seat. Valley streets — residential streets, dry hills, that flat SoCal sprawl that looks nothing like Venice. The grain is gone. The image is sharper, cleaner. Act two.

Caitlin sings casually while driving — not to camera, to the windshield, to the road. She might drum the steering wheel. He's looking at his phone or out the window. She glances at him. He doesn't look back. A small, almost invisible beat — but the camera catches it.

We don't visualize "black eye blues" or "orange bottles." The lyrics do that work. The video stays in the present tense of two people in a car. I think the less we do here the better. Just two people in a car and the silence between them. The valley does the rest.

Detail shots: overgrown hedges, a sun-bleached FOR SALE sign, a chain-link fence around a dead lawn. The valley in decay.

1:25–1:35 "41, never had a son" Digital
41, never had a son / One last chance / She's too busy having fun

They pull up to the abandoned valley house. Sun-beaten, dried out, the yard is dead. A real estate sign is face-down in the weeds.

We follow them through a gap in the fence, around the side of the house. Hero Skater finds the way in — he hops a wall easy. Caitlin struggles over it and he barely helps. She lands wrong and laughs it off. The camera should feel like it's trespassing here. Handheld, a little nervous.

The camera pushes through a dark hallway, past peeling wallpaper and dusty light, and discovers: The empty swimming pool. Drained, curved, cracked concrete. A perfect bowl. The camera holds on it for a beat. Then it opens up.

"She's too busy having fun" — Caitlin is already climbing down into the pool, exploring, touching the walls, running her hand along the curve. She's not thinking about what this place means. She's just here.

1:35–1:50 Pre-Chorus 2 Digital
They'd go all night long / Riding the rising sun / Seeing their life slip away
Now they're night-swimming laps in the ashtray / And they're sleeping all day

Hero Skater drops into the empty pool. He carves the walls. Raw and loud — board on cracked concrete, the sound echoing off the empty house. Wide and low, same framing as the Venice bowl but now in this abandoned, private space.

Caitlin jumps up and down at the edge of the pool, clapping, loving it. She's genuinely stoked — like a kid watching fireworks. Their adventure. Their secret. For a moment the energy is back to full — fun, reckless, alive.

They sit together at the edge of the pool. Sun going down — golden hour in the valley. She leans into him. He keeps watching the pool like he wants to go back in. She flicks his ear or pokes his ribs trying to get his attention. He barely reacts.

Plexiglass #2 — golden hour. She's standing over the lens, eyes closed, singing softly. Anamorphic flare streaks across the glass. Warmer than the first, but she's starting to drift.

"Night-swimming laps in the ashtray" — smoke curling through the golden-hour pool light. If we can get the smoke to catch the light right in this pool, it's going to look insane on the Alexa.

1:50–2:10 Chorus 2 — The Turn Digital
She don't wanna go / He don't wanna stay / Drinking all the time / Hairs are turning gray
She just wanna talk / He don't got nothin' to say / Smoking all the time / till they blow away

He wants to leave. She pulls his arm, tries to make him stay. Playful at first — pouty, mock-tantrumy, hanging off him like dead weight — but there's an edge to it. She's clinging and he's already halfway gone.

He leaves. Not dramatically. He just... goes. Grabs his board, heads back through the house. She watches him walk away. The camera holds on her face — first time in the video her expression isn't fun. This is the shot I'm most worried about getting right. It can't be acted. She has to actually feel something shift. We might need to run it a bunch of times or we might get it on the first take — but I'll know it when I see it.

She's alone at the house now. Sun setting. Standing at the edge of the empty pool, looking down into it.

Bouquet payoff: the bouquet finally spills. Petals scatter across the dry concrete of the pool deck. Wind catches them. Slow motion. They drift across the empty pool. They blow away. We never see them picked up. (This is the whole video in one image. If we nail this shot, the rest almost doesn't matter.)

2:10–2:35 Chorus 3 / Outro — "Blowin' away" Digital → 35mm flash → Digital
Blowin' away / Blowin' away / Blowin' away

Night. Projector on — casting color across the curved concrete walls. Cyan, magenta, warm amber. Caitlin finishes the song alone in the empty pool. Still vibing. The track still bounces. But the energy is solo now. The difference between dancing at a party and dancing alone in your room. She's not sad in here. She's just alone for the first time and finding out that's okay.

Plexiglass #3 — night. Projector light throwing color across her face from below. She looks down into the lens and her expression has changed. Not sad. Not angry. Clear. Something behind her eyes that wasn't there at midday, wasn't there at golden hour.

"Blowin' away" — the 35mm flash. One last burst. The grain floods back. Sun-drenched Venice. The skate park. Hero Skater pulling up, Caitlin glowing by his side. The boardwalk. Someone pulling her on a skateboard. Laughing. I'd love to see Caitlin standing on a skateboard being pulled slowly down the boardwalk by a crazy shirtless Venetian. Just grinning. Not cool, not posed — actually having fun. That image in 35mm with the boardwalk blowing past behind her is going to feel like a memory even while you're watching it. It's warm and beautiful and the world we started in — but we've been in the clean digital long enough that the film stock now feels like it belongs to a different day. It's the echo. The warmth comes flooding back and then it's gone.

2:35–2:40 Final Image Digital

The empty pool. Night. Projector light has gone soft. Colors bleeding across concrete. Caitlin sitting on the curved floor of the pool, alone. Knees up, arms wrapped around them. Not watching anyone. Not performing. Just sitting.

Hero Skater's board on its side nearby. Wheels still spinning. Barely. The camera is above her, looking straight down. It begins to rise. The pool widens. She gets smaller. She doesn't look up. The wheels stop. Hold one breath too long.

Black.

This isn't an ending. It's a doorway. Act three starts in the dark. Honestly if the last thing you feel is the wheels stopping, we did our job. Everything else is just getting us there.

The Arc — One Paragraph

Act one is Venice — the golden world, his world, the party. Everything is fun and loud and 35mm and she's having the best day of her life next to a boy who barely notices. Act two is the valley — the empty pool, the quiet, the moment she sees him clearly for the first time. He leaves. She stays. She finishes the song there, bathed in projector light, still vibing but solo now. One last flash of the golden Venice days hits like a wave — warm, grainy, beautiful, and unmistakably over. Then we're back in the pool. Digital. Clear. She's sitting alone. The board stops spinning. She doesn't look up. Act three starts here. We just don't get to see it.
