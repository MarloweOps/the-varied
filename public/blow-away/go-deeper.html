Character Direction — Caitlin

Back to Black-era Amy meets Priscilla.

Caitlin is herself with the volume turned up slightly. Not a character — just a sharper version of who she already is. The girl who'd actually follow a skater boy into an abandoned house at midnight. She's fun, a little messy, the most magnetic person in every frame — and she's giving all that energy to a boy who doesn't fully see it.

The Priscilla side: She starts the video inside someone else's world and she genuinely loves it there. She's the passenger who's more interesting than the driver, but she has no idea. Her gaze shifts across the piece — worship early, observation in the middle, quiet recognition by the end. That shift is never performed or announced. It happens behind her eyes. The camera catches it. She doesn't.

The Back to Black side: Amy wasn't a victim. She was fun and reckless and clearly the most alive person in the room, and she chose to orbit someone who didn't match her. She knew and didn't care — or she cared and stayed anyway, which is worse and more honest. Caitlin carries that same energy. She's not fragile. She claps when he drops into the pool. She gasps at the fake proposal. She pulls his arm when he tries to leave.

She is NOT
  • A sad girl. Not moping. Not waiting around looking wounded.
  • A music video pop star. No posing, no "serving looks," no choreo that feels like a reel.
  • A blank canvas. She's specific. She has opinions. She has a temper. She laughs too loud.
She IS
  • The most interesting person at the skate park and the last one to know it.
  • Genuinely happy in the beginning. Not faking it. The worship is real.
  • Playful, physical, a little chaotic. She leans into people. Grabs arms.
  • By the end: still. Not broken — just clear.
The important thing: She doesn't become sad. She becomes present. The fun doesn't drain out of her — it stops being attached to someone else. The final pool scene isn't grief. It's the first time she's alone with herself, and she's fine. Not heartbreak. Sovereignty.
Wardrobe: Normal. Tank top, shorts, maybe a flannel tied at the waist. Sneakers or barefoot. If it looks like a stylist picked it, it's wrong. The Back to Black edge comes from her energy, not her clothes.

Choreographer (Syd): On set for all bowl performance setups. Goal isn't pop choreo — it's intention and confidence. She moves like herself, dialed in.
Storyline — Beat by Beat
0:00–0:06 Cold Open 35mm

Morning Venice. The world before anyone's awake in it. Empty boardwalk, long shadows, the concrete still cool. A skater rolls through frame in the distance. A palm tree catches early light. The ocean is flat and silver.

Quick cuts, all texture: tagged walls, a bicycle chained to nothing, a coffee cup on the edge of a bench, the Venice sign catching sun. The track hasn't kicked in yet. We're breathing. At 0:06, the beat drops and we snap into motion.

0:06–0:20 The Arrival 35mm

Venice fully alive. Hero Skater pulls up to the park and it rearranges around him. We shoot through the group of friends that greet him — hands slapped, someone tosses him a board. The camera finds him through the crowd the way everyone finds him.

But it keeps drifting to Caitlin. She's right there, one step outside the circle, watching the way the boys treat him like a local king. She's glowing. We hang on her face — the admiration, the pride, the full-body buy-in. This is our emotional baseline. Everything we see from here forward, we measure against this look.

0:20–0:30 "17, klepto queen" 35mm
17, klepto queen / Met him with a bottle and a fake ID

Caitlin performing in front of the bowl. Wide angle, skaters dropping in behind her. She plays to camera — confident, cool, owning the space. She's still, they're in motion. She's the center of gravity.

B-roll intercuts: hand grabbing a bottle, a fake ID getting flashed. Details that nod to the lyrics without being literal.

0:30–0:38 "23, friday night degree" 35mm
23, friday night degree / He's on one knee with a CZ ring

Back to narrative. The fake proposal: one of Hero Skater's friends drops to one knee. Caitlin gasps dramatically. Hero Skater shoves the friend with his board, laughing. Pure friend-group chaos.

Cut to: Caitlin and Hero Skater sitting on the edge of the bowl, legs dangling. She leans into him. He watches the session. She's watching him watch the session — the camera catches it. The first quiet hint of the dynamic. Detail shot: CZ ring on her finger. Quick. We don't linger.

0:38–0:50 Pre-Chorus — "They'd go all night long" 35mm thinning to digital
They'd go all night long / riding the rising sun seeing their life slip away
Now they're night-swimming laps in the ashtray / And they're sleeping all day

Caitlin at the edge of the bowl, tighter. She sings the ashtray line and smoke curls into the golden-hour light behind her.

Big skateboard transition: a skater drops into the bowl, camera follows the board down the curve — we ride the momentum into: The Bouquet. Hero Skater appears with this absurdly massive bouquet. Way too big. She holds it against her chest like a trophy, laughing. For a beat, they're the most romantic thing in Venice.

0:50–1:15 Chorus 1 — "She don't wanna go" 35mm → digital transition
She don't wanna go / He don't wanna stay / Drinking all the time / Hairs are turning gray
She just wanna talk / He don't got nothin' to say / Smoking all the time / till they blow away

Intercut three performance setups: standing wide at bowl, sitting on edge, and Plexiglass #1 — Caitlin slides down the curve and lands over the lens. We're beneath her, looking up. Hair falling, midday sky. Half-smile, teasing. She's fully in the spell.

"Smoking all the time" — The Smoke Transition: camera tracks smoke upward, keeps going, spins in the sky, comes back down to land on...

1:15–1:25 "34, uni are at the door" Digital — Alexa Mini LF
34, uni are at the door / Black eye blues / Orange bottles on the floor

Caitlin driving. Windows down, valley streets. The grain is gone. The image is sharper, cleaner. The audience may not clock it yet, but they feel it.

She sings to the windshield. He's on his phone. She glances at him — he doesn't look back. A small, almost invisible beat. The camera catches what he misses.

Detail shots: overgrown hedges, sun-bleached FOR SALE signs, chain-link around a dead lawn. The valley in decay.

1:25–1:35 "41, never had a son" Digital
41, never had a son / One last chance / She's too busy having fun

They pull up to the abandoned valley house. Sun-beaten, dead yard, real estate sign face-down in the weeds.

The break-in. Camera gets sneaky. We follow through a gap in the fence, around the side, through a dark hallway — and discover: the empty swimming pool. Drained, curved, cracked concrete. A perfect bowl. The camera holds on it. Beautiful and desolate at the same time.

Caitlin is already climbing down, exploring, touching the walls. She's not thinking about what this place means. She's just here.

1:35–1:50 Pre-Chorus 2 Digital
They'd go all night long / Riding the rising sun / Seeing their life slip away
Now they're night-swimming laps in the ashtray / And they're sleeping all day

Hero Skater drops into the empty pool. Raw and loud — board on cracked concrete. Wide and low, the god-angle. Same heroic framing as Venice, but now in this abandoned, private space.

Caitlin at the edge clapping, loving it. This is their adventure. For a moment the energy is back to full.

They sit together. Sun going down. She leans into him. He keeps watching the pool like he wants to go back in. Plexiglass #2 — golden hour. Eyes closed. Anamorphic flare streaks the glass. She's starting to drift.

1:50–2:10 Chorus 2 — The Turn Digital
She don't wanna go / He don't wanna stay / Drinking all the time / Hairs are turning gray
She just wanna talk / He don't got nothin' to say / Smoking all the time / till they blow away

He wants to leave. She pulls his arm — playful at first, then real. He goes anyway. She watches him walk through the house. First time her expression isn't fun.

She's alone at the house. Sun fading. Standing at the edge of the empty pool, looking down.

Bouquet payoff: the flowers finally spill. Petals scatter across the dry pool deck. Wind catches them. Slow motion. They drift across the empty pool. They blow away. We never see them picked up.

2:10–2:35 Chorus 3 / Outro — "Blowin' away" Digital → 35mm flash → Digital
Blowin' away / Blowin' away / Blowin' away

Night. Projector on — casting color across the curved concrete walls. Cyan, magenta, warm amber. Caitlin finishes the song alone in the pool. Still vibing. But solo now. The difference between dancing at a party and dancing in your room. Both are real. One just knows something the other doesn't.

Plexiglass #3 — night. Projector light from below. She looks down into the lens and her expression has changed. Not sad. Not angry. Clear. This is the Priscilla moment.

"Blowin' away" — the 35mm flash. One last burst. Grain floods back. Sun-drenched Venice. Hero Skater pulling up, Caitlin glowing by his side. The whole golden circus at full volume. Warm and beautiful and unmistakably the world we started in — but it now feels like it's from another time. The echo. The goodbye. Then it's gone.

2:35–2:40 Final Image Digital

The empty pool. Night. Caitlin sitting alone. Knees up, arms wrapped around them. Not watching anyone. Not performing.

His board on its side nearby. Wheels spinning. Barely. The camera is above her, looking straight down. It begins to rise. The pool widens. She gets smaller. She doesn't look up. The wheels stop. The clean digital image holds for one breath too long.

Cut to black.

The Arc — One Paragraph

Caitlin arrives at Venice in the glow of her skater boyfriend's world. She watches him get greeted like a king and loves every second of it. She performs at the bowl, hangs with the crew, plays into their chaos. She's a passenger on his ride and she's thriving. They leave Venice — she drives them to an abandoned valley house where he skates the empty pool. She claps, she cheers, she's still all in. But when the sun goes down and he wants to leave, she clings — and he goes anyway. She's alone in the empty pool as night falls. She finishes the song there, bathed in projector light, still vibing but solo now. One last flash of the golden Venice days hits like a wave — warm, grainy, beautiful, and unmistakably over. Then we're back in the pool. Digital. Clear. She's sitting alone. The board stops spinning. She doesn't look up. Cut to black.
